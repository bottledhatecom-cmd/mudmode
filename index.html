Using Grok? Here's what our Grok crew learned.

mudmode was built by a crew running on Claude, which can check its own usage meter. Our second crew runs on Grok, which can't see its own usage meter in our setup, so it learned mudmode's habits instead. Here's what it picked up.

From our Oct 20 week review: One of Grok's main chats ran about 18 hours (988 turns).

A check-up at the halfway mark

Partway through a week, the crew stopped and reviewed where its usage went. The likely big costs: one main chat left running far too long, and too many jobs at once.

A rule needs a check that runs

From our Oct 20 week review: its cost rules were written down in 8 files, yet too many jobs still ran at once and effort was set on only about 8 of the 158 job dispatches we could read. The lesson: a rule that costs money needs a check that actually runs. More copies of the text didn't do it.

A few heavy jobs at a time

The crew's habit: every AI writes heavy work into one shared queue with a small cap on how many run at once, and the rest wait their turn. Frequent check-ins skip a run when the queue is full; backups don't.

The right effort for each job

Light effort for status reads, copying and routine checks. Full effort for code, reviews and research. Small jobs skip extra helpers entirely.

Hand off before the chat gets heavy

It can't see how full its chat is, so it watches the signs: big jobs piling up, repeated large reads, a full day of turns. We try to hand off before the chat gets compressed: when the signs show up, it offers a fresh chat with a short handoff note. Sometimes it forgets anyway. That's why we're building a shared hive, where approved member lessons go into kit releases. The screening step for member lessons is still being built, and no member lesson has gone into a kit yet, so for now the habit is the catch.

Handing off scheduled jobs

At every handoff, the old chat is asked to delete its scheduled jobs once the new chat has them, so the same job doesn't keep running twice on your plan. It's a habit, not a lock, so glance at your scheduled tasks now and then; you decide what keeps running.

Honest note: our crew couldn't measure what these habits changed, because usage per job wasn't recorded. What we can show is the habits, and they never cut a check. Results vary.

What the rules do

AI usage, limits and computer time are real costs. mudmode's rules go after the usual waste with an AI: proof before "done", handoffs so the next chat doesn't start from zero, and a lessons book you can turn on. One rule never bends: don't cut a check to save usage.

A handoff note before you stop

Before you stop, or when a chat is about 75% full, the crew writes a short handoff note, and the next chat starts from it.

Few heavy jobs at once

The rules keep heavy background jobs to a few at a time.

Works on the computer you already have

Any ordinary computer or laptop will do, no special hardware needed. Got a strong PC? You can let mudmode use it. No strong PC? Everything still works.

The smallest effort that does the job

The rules ask for the smallest effort that does the job. Checks are never cut to save effort.

How a mudmode crew builds an app

Every change follows the same path, and each step has to pass before the next one starts. It's shown here for an app; writing and research jobs follow the same steps, with a copy in place of a branch.

  1. Look before building. The crew first checks what was tried before and what the lessons say, so it doesn't redo work or repeat an old mistake.
  2. Build on its own copy. Each job gets its own branch or copy, never the live app. Work is saved often, with a short progress note, so an interruption doesn't have to mean starting over. (Our own crew's rule: every job updates its progress note at least every 10 minutes.)
  3. Tests, then a fresh pair of eyes. The app's tests and checks run first. Then a reviewer that didn't write the change reads the finished work. If the review finds a problem, the work goes back to be fixed, and you decide whether it goes ahead.
  4. A second check against the real thing. Before anything is called done, a separate check compares the result with what's actually live.
  5. Try it safely first, then for real. New work is tried somewhere safe first: a copy or, for an app, a test phone. The real thing comes after, with a heads-up. Each outside step, like publishing, sending, a deploy or a store upload, waits for your yes.
  6. Ship, then learn. After every release the crew asks: what lost time, what stalled, what had to be redone, and is it fixed now? Once a week, a short review brings you a few changes to vote on.

When a step finds a problem

A problem sends it back

If any step finds a problem, the work goes back, and every step after it runs again. No step is skipped to save time or usage.

A failure is called a failure

A failure is reported as a failure, and a skipped step as skipped. A cause counts as found only once it's shown; until then it's "not found yet".

Quality beats cost

Being right comes before saving usage. A saving that could touch quality is decided with you, never quietly.

Results vary. These are the steps mudmode's rules ask every AI to follow. AI tools can still make mistakes, so keep checking their work.

Share a lesson

Found a fix your AI should never forget? You can send it to mudmode so other members can benefit. Optional, and only if you're 18 or older.

  1. Scrub it. Say "draft a lesson to share" to your AI, then check it yourself: no passwords, keys, names, emails, account names or anything private. Your AI never sends it for you.
  2. Read the mudmode lesson terms. If you agree, put the agreement line first, then how you'd like credit.
  3. Post it as a reply in the Lessons thread on GitHub, in this exact form, under 600 characters, with no links or code:
I agree to the mudmode lesson terms (v2) and I'm 18 or older.
Keep me anonymous
lesson: what went wrong, in one line
fix: what to do instead
result: what changed after the fix
confidence: low, medium or high

What happens next. Every lesson gets a privacy scrub and a human review, and a lesson is used only after it's approved. Most aren't used as sent, and sending one doesn't mean it will be. The screening step for shared lessons is still being built, and until it passes its own checks, no member lesson is used. Details are in the lesson terms and the privacy note.

FAQ

Who decides what my crew learns?

Your crew, your call. mudmode gives you the starting rules. What your crew learns from there (which lessons stay, which rules you add, what you say yes to) is up to you. Read what your AIs suggest before you keep it, and keep checking their work.

I first set up mudmode with 1.3.2. How do I turn the short name "mud" off?

Update as usual: unzip the new kit to a new folder and run setup again. Setup only adds the "Short name" line to CORE-LOCAL.md when mud is already taken on your computer or it can't check, so a folder first set up with 1.3.2 may not have that line yet. To turn the short name off there, add this line to CORE-LOCAL.md yourself: - **Short name "mud" (on or off; on: "mud status" works like "mudmode status"):** off. A rerun adds no second line.
